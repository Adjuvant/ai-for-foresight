# Weak Signals

Theory lineage of weak-signal detection from Ansoff (1975) through Hiltunen's 'future sign' to the 2021 Futures construct review; LLM-era adaptation is early and pilot-scale, with no verified deployed 'LLM weak-signal detector' as of 2026.

## Key Claims
- The weak-signal concept originates with Ansoff's 1975 'Managing Strategic Surprise by Response to Weak Signals', which framed weak signals as a response mechanism for strategic surprise [15] [[Literature/agentic-signal-intelligence#^agentic-signal-intelligence-15]].
- Hiltunen's 'future sign' model, defined by three dimensions, remains a core construct in weak-signal and futures research [16] [[Literature/agentic-signal-intelligence#^agentic-signal-intelligence-16]].
- LLM-era weak-signal research is exploratory and pilot-scale: TForMIX (IEEE Access 2025) and a fully prospective SSRN venture tournament (2026) benchmark LLM foresight, but no verified deployed 'LLM weak-signal detector' product has surfaced [18][19] [[Literature/agentic-signal-intelligence#^agentic-signal-intelligence-18]] [[Literature/agentic-signal-intelligence#^agentic-signal-intelligence-19]].
- Ansoff (1975) framed weak signals as a five-state knowledge ladder — sense of threat/opportunity, source identified, threat concrete, response concrete, outcome concrete — enabling 'graduated response through amplification' before information is adequate for conventional strategic planning [11] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-11]].
- The 2021 unifying review (van Veen & Ortt) distils 68 weak-signal definitions into one construct — a perception of strategic phenomena distant to the perceiver's frame of reference — with four graded weakness levels; 'the essence of weakness is the lack of knowledge of the perceiver about a signal' [39] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-39]] [13] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-13]].
- The same review documents an emergence bias: keywords like 'peripheral' and 'outside frame' appear in weak-signal definitions only after 2004, implying most studies operationalised 'weak' as merely 'less strong' and skewed the literature toward stronger signals [13] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-13]].
- LLM-era weak-signal work is pilot/benchmark scale (BERTrend noise/weak/strong topic classification; the SSRN fully prospective ranking; SCANAR/AIDOC ~62% manual-review reduction at 95% recall), and the OECD/WEF practitioner survey reports AI remains weak on inductive reasoning about unknown low-probability signals — no verified deployed 'LLM weak-signal detector' as of mid-2026 [41] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-41]] [15] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-15]] [18] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-18]] [19] [[Literature/foresight-automation-weak-signals#^foresight-automation-weak-signals-19]].
