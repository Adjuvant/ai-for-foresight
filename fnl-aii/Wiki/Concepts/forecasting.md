# Forecasting

Methods for producing falsifiable predictions about the future from human judgement and statistics — expert surveys, Delphi, prediction markets, superforecasters, judgmental bootstrapping, statistical and ML forecasting; Kohler (2021) reviews the family and finds forecasting skill is task- and environment-dependent, forecastability degrades with horizon, and track records are what enable systematic evaluation.

## Key Claims
- Expert intuition can be trusted only in high-validity environments with stable relationships between objectively identifiable cues and subsequent events or outcomes, such as medicine and firefighting; forecasting the future value of individual stocks or long-term political events takes place in near zero-validity environments (per Kahneman and Klein 2009). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Forecasting contains clear elements of skill: the correlation between forecaster performance in one year and the next was 0.65 in the Good Judgement Project, and about 70% of superforecasters maintained their status year-to-year (per Tetlock and Gardner 2016). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- An aggregate prediction by top AI experts for when AI systems would achieve human-level performance on all tasks was substantially earlier than their forecast for parity on the narrower task of conducting AI research, even though the latter is logically a subset of the former — evidence that expert survey results can shift with question framing (per Grace et al. 2018). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- The first three M-competitions found that more sophisticated and complex forecasting methods did not improve accuracy; in the M4 competition, pure machine learning fared fairly poorly while hybrid approaches combining traditional statistics with elements of machine learning performed well (per Makridakis et al.). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Prediction markets have produced forecasts with lower prediction errors than conventional forecasting techniques, but their efficiency is undermined by limited liquidity, regulatory complications, and incentives to manipulate them when they enter public discussion. ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- The capacity of forecasting and user-insight studies to realistically and plausibly account for what people's future needs might be is limited for emerging technologies that have not reached markets or become ubiquitous, because people learn to live with such technologies through evolving everyday experiences rather than reaching a fixed acceptance end state (per Lindgren, Pink and Fors 2021). ([[Raw/pinkDesignAnthropologicalForesighting2023]])
