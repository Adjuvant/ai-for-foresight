# Trend Analysis

Analysis of established ongoing dynamics — statistical trends, structural shifts, fads, countertrends and megatrends — including their causes and impacts; Kohler (2021) reviews trend identification (STEEP, data sources), trend impact analysis and the contested megatrends industry, while cautioning that expert event prediction degrades beyond a five-year horizon and that CBRN trend analysis must look beyond annual impacts.

## Key Claims
- The accuracy of expert predictions in geopolitics and economics declines towards being no better than chance for five years and beyond, so longer-term futures work should target structural factors that influence the future independent of individuals and events (per Tetlock and Gardner 2016, building on Braudel 1949). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Megatrend publications are driven more by marketing than methodology: the term is inadequately defined, most books on the topic offer little methodology for trend selection, and the apparent authority of identified trends is a symbolically (not substantively) powerful resource sold to anyone seeking a competitive edge (per Slaughter 1993). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- A claimed trend should be backed up by a relevant indicator or index to distinguish it from conjecture; a complex trend such as the rise of China cannot be fully captured by a single indicator or index. ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Causal layered analysis is a misnomer for a layered qualitative discourse analysis: its lower layers (worldviews, metaphors and myths) are not the key causes of developments on the upper layers, so it cannot identify systemic structures that drive long-term technological trends (method per Inayatullah 1998). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Because outliers are likely to create most consequences, trend analysis in CBRN contexts should not be based on annual or decadal impacts of accidents and attacks, but on factors such as proliferation, price-performance changes of new tools, access to materials, near-events, spillover events, and capacities to respond. ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
