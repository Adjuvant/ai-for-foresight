# Science Fiction Planning

Defence organisations commission or methodize science fiction as a planning instrument — institutionally-commissioned anthologies and writers' rooms (NATO *Visions of Warfare 2036*, France's Red Team Défense, UK Dstl "useful fiction", US Army Mad Scientist) alongside a methodized lineage from science-fiction prototyping through threatcasting to "fictional intelligence" (FICINT) — with the impact evidence base lagging the enthusiasm.

## Key Claims
- Institutionally-commissioned science fiction became an explicit defence planning instrument across 2015–2026 — NATO ACT's *Visions of Warfare 2036* anthology, France's Red Team Défense writers' room (2019–2023), UK Dstl's commissioned 'useful fiction' and 2026 Creative Futures anthology, and the US Army Mad Scientist Laboratory's fiction contests and scenario posts — with mixed public exposure from classified outputs to published books [1][3][5][7] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-1]] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-3]] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-5]] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-7]].
- The practice hardened from Brian David Johnson's Intel-era science fiction prototyping into the peer-reviewed threatcasting framework fielded with the US Army (2016–2018) and, by 2025, into 'fictional intelligence' (FICINT), engaging every US service plus NATO and Five Eyes partners [6][8] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-6]] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-8]].
- No verified evaluation shows any commissioned SF program changed doctrine or procurement; the sole peer-reviewed audit found NATO's twelve *Visions of Warfare 2036* narratives 'strikingly similar' to one another, proposing improved SF scenario cards instead [2] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-2]].
- French scholarly commentary frames Red Team Défense as a wager on science fiction's 'dysperformativity' — SF may fail to produce strategic effect — and warns that SF plausibly steers decision-makers toward false threats [4] [[Literature/science-fiction-military-planning#^science-fiction-military-planning-4]].
- Design fiction uses fictional artifacts and scenarios to provide an 'anti-environment' that reduces or reverses pre-existing intuitions, helping individuals and organizations focus on first principles; it is particularly suited to normative questions because human moral reasoning is often a post hoc rationalization of pre-existing feelings (per Dunne and Raby 2013; Haidt 2001). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Science-fiction prototyping follows a five-step recipe — pick the technology, science or issue; embed it in a world with people and locations; introduce scientific inflection points; explore ramifications and the human inflection point; extract lessons for the present (per Johnson 2011). ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
- Fiction-based methods (design fiction, science-fiction prototyping) sit within the scenario planner's toolkit alongside morphological boxes, classic matrices and backcasting, and can also serve as a playful way to explore scientific laws. ([[Raw/kohlerStrategicForesightKnowledge2021]])
