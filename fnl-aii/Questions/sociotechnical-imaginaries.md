# Sociotechnical Imaginaries: Search and Research Report

Open research question for phase 1 (deep research → `outputs/` → `Research/AI Governance & Ethics/`). Split into two deliverables: **A — sociotechnical-imaginaries literature search** (landscape map), **B — research report** (verified synthesis with construct-validity-bounded scope).

Seed source and vault anchor: Pink, Sarah; Strengers, Yolande; Dahlgren, Kari; Korsmeyer, Hannah (2023). *Design anthropological foresighting: Reframing automated futures*, Futures 154, 103275 `[[Raw/pinkDesignAnthropologicalForesighting2023]]` — uses sociotechnical imaginaries (per Jasanoff 2015) as the foil for design anthropological foresighting: dominant industry and government future visions whose promissory claims are argued to be implausible against everyday-life practice.

## Primary question

> What is the verified state of knowledge on **sociotechnical imaginaries** — collectively held, institutionally stabilised, and publicly performed visions of desirable futures, animated by shared understandings of social life and social order attainable through advances in science and technology (per Jasanoff and Kim 2009, 2015) — as it (a) is defined and delimited in the scholarly literature, (b) operates in empirical domains including energy, automation and AI, (c) functions within and constrains **strategic foresight practice**, and (d) is being stabilised or contested in the LLM/agentic era, and what material effects can be verified?

## Construct validity scope (anti-over-scoping guard)

The construct is **sociotechnical imaginaries**, not "technology visions in general". The definitional core to protect: imaginaries are *collective* (not individual), *institutionally stabilised* (not ephemeral), *publicly performed* (not private), *normative* (visions of desirable social order, not neutral forecasts), and *materially consequential* (they shape investment, regulation, infrastructure). Every sub-question below maps to one operational facet of this construct and to an observable evidence class. Search and report must stay inside these bounds:

| In scope (facets of the construct) | Adjacent constructs — OUT of scope unless shown to be a mechanism of the core construct |
|---|---|
| Definitional core and lineage: Jasanoff & Kim (2009, 2015); differentiation from determinism, solutionism, expectations | Technological determinism (autonomous-force account of technology's social effects — a *contrast* construct, already carried on `[[Wiki/Concepts/strategic-foresight]]`) |
| Empirical operationalisation: energy futures, automation/smart home, AI, autonomous vehicles, biotech, nuclear (per the founding atom/nation studies) | Psychological or individual "images of the future" (Polak; personal mental imagery) — *individual* vs *collective* cognition |
| Foresight-practice interaction: imaginaries as critique object (Pink et al.), as stabilised inputs to scenario/visioning, industry report analysis, government strategy documents | Technological solutionism (Morozov 2013: the fallacy that every problem has a tech fix — an *attitude/argument*, not an institutionally performed vision, though often a mechanism within imaginaries) |
| Institutional stabilisation and contestation: how imaginaries are performed, embedded in reports/consultations/policy, and challenged by counter-evidence (everyday practice, ethnographic findings) | Innovation-studies "promissory narratives"/expectations (van Lente, Borup et al.: expectations *within* innovation processes — adjacent but analytically distinct from collectively held visions of social order) |
| Material consequences: verified links from imaginaries to investment, regulation, standards, infrastructure, design lock-in | STS power critique generally (imaginaries analysis is one lens; do not absorb the whole STS corpus) |
| AI-era delta (2022–2026): dominant imaginaries of AI/automation, their institutional carriers (industry, government, consultancies), and LLM-generated or agentic-era future visions | Media science-fiction analysis of imaginary futures (film/literature) absent institutional carriers in policy/industry |

Rule: a source enters the report only if it bears on **collective, institutionally stabilised, publicly performed visions of desirable futures tied to science/technology** and their contestation or material effects; adjacent constructs serve only as contrast cases or mechanisms. This is the explicit boundary that keeps the question from over-scoping into "all STS" or "all futures discourse".

## Sub-questions (each bounded, each falsifiable by source evidence)

1. **Landscape (search deliverable).** What is the scholarly lineage and definitional core of sociotechnical imaginaries from Jasanoff & Kim (2009, 2015) to 2026: the canonical statements, the definitional boundaries drawn against determinism/solutionism/expectations, and the main critiques or extensions of the concept?
2. **Empirical operationalisation.** In which domains has the concept been operationalised (nuclear power, energy, smart home, autonomous driving, AI, biotech, space), and what *verified* empirical findings show imaginaries at work — including documented failures of dominant imaginaries against everyday practice (smart home non-adoption, autonomous-driving delays)?
3. **Foresight interaction.** How do sociotechnical imaginaries function within strategic foresight: as objects of critique and reframing (design anthropological foresighting per Pink et al. 2023), as stabilised inputs or blind spots in industry report analysis, scenario and visioning work, and what do documented foresight engagements reveal about how imaginaries are institutionally stabilised and contested?
4. **Governance and materiality.** What verified evidence links sociotechnical imaginaries to material outcomes — investment allocation, regulation, standards, infrastructure, design — and what remains unverified (e.g., the reframing-concepts claim that evidence-based alternatives can correct dominant visions that underpin sectoral planning)?
5. **AI-era delta.** What are the dominant contemporaneous sociotechnical imaginaries of AI and automation (2022–2026), who are their institutional carriers (industry, government bodies, consultancies, standards organisations), and what verified research addresses whether LLM-generated or agentic-era future visions are stabilising or fragmenting them?
