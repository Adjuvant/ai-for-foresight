# No Evaluated Programs: The SF Impact Evidence Gap

## Context

RQ2 of this project. The science-fiction-military-planning scan surfaced a striking asymmetry: institutional enthusiasm for commissioned SF versus an empty evaluation record. This memo captures the observation mid-research, before phase 3 designs the score.

## Observation

The vault's scan verified eight sources and found no peer-reviewed evaluation showing any commissioned SF program changed doctrine or procurement. The closest audit (Hiltunen & Huhtinen 2024) found NATO's twelve *Visions of Warfare 2036* narratives "strikingly similar" — a convergence finding, not an effect measurement. Meanwhile institutional communications are boosterish (Dstl's chief scientific adviser: "Fiction gives us the freedom to explore those scenarios in ways traditional analysis can't"). Booster framing is also structural in the methods literature: threatcasting's utility claims are inventor-reported.

## Interpretation

This tests two readings of [[Wiki/Concepts/foresight-evaluation]]. One reading — evaluation designs exist but failure modes dominate — does not fit: for narrative futures work the problem is prior — there is no evaluation infrastructure at all (no dated outputs, no resolution criteria, no independent audit tradition). The stronger reading, consistent with the evidence gap on [[Wiki/Concepts/science-fiction-planning]], is that narrative intervention programs have been adopted on rhetorical promise, and the only transferable design is process metrics (response capture — the Map2 loop familiar from experiential-futures practice).

## Open Questions

- Is "strikingly similar narratives" a validity failure of the program or an intrinsic property of commissioned anthologies?
- Would improved SF scenario cards (Hiltunen & Huhtinen's proposal) be evaluable under any existing design?
