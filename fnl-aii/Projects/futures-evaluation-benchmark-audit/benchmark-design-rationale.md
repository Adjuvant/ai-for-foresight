# Benchmark Design Rationale: Readiness Scoring Without New Experiments

## Context

RQ3: can evaluation readiness be scored cheaply from existing verified claims, without new field experiments? This note records why the audit is designed as a desk exercise over the vault's verified claims rather than a benchmark construction effort.

## Rationale

Three decisions. First, no new forecast corpus: the verified contamination finding (1–45% across six benchmarks with up to +14% accuracy inflation) makes constructing a comparative benchmark costly and leakage-prone; reusing existing verified claims avoids that failure mode at the source. Second, readiness scoring over program properties: because commissioned SF programs lack dated, resolvable outputs, the only honest metric is a checklist of preconditions (public corpus, response capture, independent audit, decision linkage). Third, provenance tiering: medium-verification sources are enough to score readiness but must be labelled as such, since none of the underlying impact claims have been independently replicated (see the science-fiction-planning evidence gap).

## Load-bearing Constraints

- No new forecast corpus may be built in this phase (contamination risk per the verified benchmark findings).
- Partially classified program outputs (Red Team Défense seasons, some NATO material) bound what can be audited from the public record.
- No verified ground truth exists linking any SF program to doctrine or procurement, so the score cannot be validated for accuracy — only for completeness of preconditions.

## Open Questions

- Does the readiness score correlate with anything measurable, given no program has been evaluated?
- Should the score be weighted, or is a plain precondition checklist more defensible?
