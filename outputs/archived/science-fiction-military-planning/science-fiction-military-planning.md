## Quick Synthesis

The domain splits into two streams. First, **institutionally commissioned science fiction**: NATO Allied Command Transformation's *Visions of Warfare 2036* anthology (2016), France's Red Team Défense writers' room (2019–2023, seasons 0–3), the UK Dstl's commissioned "useful fiction" (2023 Singer & Cole; a 2026 anthology), and the US Army's Mad Scientist Laboratory with its science-fiction writing contest and fictional scenario posts. These are planning instruments with mixed public exposure — some outputs classified, some published as books [1][3][5][7].

Second, a **methodized practice lineage**: Brian David Johnson's science-fiction prototyping (Intel, 2011) hardened into the peer-reviewed threatcasting framework with the US Army (2016–2018), institutionalized at ASU's Threatcasting Lab and producing graphic-novella "science fiction prototypes" for the Army Cyber Institute (2019) [6]; meanwhile Singer & Cole's *Ghost Fleet* (2015) became reading-list fodder across US services and evolved into a formalized "fictional intelligence" (FICINT) practice engaging every US service, NATO and Five Eyes partners by 2025 [8].

Across 2015–2026 the practice moved from novelty to embeddedness, but the **evidence base lags the enthusiasm**: the one peer-reviewed audit of a NATO SF program found its twelve narratives "strikingly similar" to one another [2], French scholarly commentary interrogates the "dysperformative" wager of SF for strategy [4], and no verified evaluation shows any SF program changed doctrine or procurement. Grey-literature warnings that SF may steer decision-makers toward "false threats" recur in the French-language debate [4].

## Patterns and Themes

**Two distinct institutional modes.** Commissioned anthologies/seasoned writers' rooms (NATO 2016, France 2019–2023, Dstl 2023/2026) sit alongside methodized futures practice (threatcasting, SFP, FICINT). The former produces narrative products; the latter produces repeatable processes — and the same people straddle both (August Cole co-edited the NATO anthology and co-wrote the Dstl commission; Johnson's lab staff recur in JFutures experiential-futures publications) [1][6][7][8].

**2015–2026 arc: from novelty to embedded practice.** *Ghost Fleet* (2015) → Pentagon reading lists and NSC briefings by 2016 → CIA *Studies in Intelligence* review (2016) → FICINT contracting by 2025 with all US services, NATO, Five Eyes [8]. Dstl's 2023 commission and 2026 anthology extend the same arc in the UK [7]. France's program, by contrast, appears closed: public site static since ~2024 and Season 3 (June 2023) declared final [3].

**The evidence gap (honest flag).** No peer-reviewed evaluation found showing any SF program changed doctrine or procurement [2][3][6]. The nearest thing to an audit (Hiltunen & Huhtinen 2024) is mildly damning — narrative homogeny [2]. The French critique corpus (Michaud 2023/2025, Roussie 2025) explicitly warns about overestimating SF's predictive power and steering toward "fausses menaces" (false threats) [4]. Booster framing is structural: method originators (Johnson) and program insiders (Colas, RTD writer) co-author the positive literature [4][6].

**Vault date correction (medium-verification note).** The vault's `candyTurningForesightOut2025` item appears misdated: the verified publication is Candy & Kornet, "Turning Foresight Inside Out," *Journal of Futures Studies* 23(3), **March 2019** — no 2025 re-issue was found in indexes or on Candy's blog. Same corpus (JFS "Design and Futures" special issue) includes a Strategic Foresight Studio paper co-authored by ASU Threatcasting Lab staff, tying experiential futures to the defence-adjacent lineage [6].

**Deliberate demarcations.** Adjacent agenda-style foresight (NATO 2030, NIC *Global Trends 2040*, RAND "Security 2040") is narrative scenario work, not fiction, and was excluded from the numbered sources; IARPA's FUSE program and RAND's RR-A198-4 were checked and contain no fiction content. Claims circulating about a "Ghost Fleet" named military exercise or Naval War College wargame could not be verified and are treated as unverified [1][3][8].
